# Thesis Intel — 2026-05-11

## TL;DR (3 bullets)
- Document AI is shifting from “best OCR” to measurable, task-level eval suites (latency + accuracy + error taxonomy) as buyers demand proof.
- Voice AI infra is converging on “fully streaming” stacks (token streaming + codec streaming + realtime transport) where tail latency becomes the differentiator.
- Insurtech opportunity is in workflow-native copilots (claims/renewals/endorsements) with auditable extraction, permissions, and human-in-the-loop controls.

## Hallazgos (6–10)
1) Extend: tooling focus is moving to Document AI evaluation/benchmarking
   - Why it matters: procurement will increasingly require reproducible evals (datasets, metrics, failure buckets), not vendor demos.
   - Links:
     - https://www.extend.ai/resources/best-evaluation-benchmarking-tools-document-ai
   - Actionable idea for Brokia: ship an “Eval Mode” for policy/endorsement ingestion, with golden sets + diff reports per model/vendor.

2) Mixpeek curated rankings highlight platform fragmentation in Document AI
   - Why it matters: “platform sprawl” creates integration risk, and a chance to be the orchestrator layer.
   - Links:
     - https://mixpeek.com/curated-lists/best-document-ai-platforms
   - Actionable idea for Brokia: build a pluggable extraction adapter layer (vendor-agnostic) plus routing by doc type and confidence.

3) OCR leaderboards and production-stack posts emphasize end-to-end constraints (layout, tables, scans)
   - Why it matters: insurance docs fail on tables, stamps, and multi-column layouts, which drive downstream hallucination risk.
   - Links:
     - https://instavar.com/blog/ai-production-stack/OCR_SOTA_Feb_2026_Open_Document_AI_Leaderboard
   - Actionable idea for Brokia: add doc preflight (quality score, skew/noise, table density) to decide OCR path and required human review.

4) camb.ai guide: realtime streaming TTS as a baseline capability, not a premium feature
   - Why it matters: voice agents feel “broken” above ~300–500ms perceived delays; infra choices become product choices.
   - Links:
     - https://www.camb.ai/blog-post/real-time-tts-api-for-low-latency-speech-streaming
   - Actionable idea for Brokia: prototype a voice-driven FNOL/claims intake with strict latency SLOs (p95, p99) and fallback to text.

5) respond.io: WhatsApp voice agents are maturing into operational workflows (handoffs, compliance, routing)
   - Why it matters: distribution is where brokers live; voice + WhatsApp is a direct channel for renewals, reminders, and intake.
   - Links:
     - https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-ai-voice-agent
   - Actionable idea for Brokia: test WhatsApp “quote follow-up” voice agent that only answers from approved knowledge + captures structured fields.

6) Gradium latency benchmarking: buyers are starting to compare TTFA and tail latency across vendors
   - Why it matters: measurable latency benchmarks will shape vendor selection and unit economics.
   - Links:
     - https://gradium.ai/content/tts-latency-benchmark-2026
   - Actionable idea for Brokia: create an internal benchmark harness (your prompts, your languages) and bake vendor swaps into config.

## Ideas accionables para Brokia (3–5)
- Implementar un “Extraction QA pack”: golden docs + métricas (field F1, table accuracy, time-to-first-token, confidence calibration).
- Diseñar un copilot de renovaciones con guardrails: permisos por rol, trazabilidad (qué fuente sustentó cada respuesta), y HITL obligatorio bajo umbral.
- Piloto de WhatsApp (texto + voz) para recolección estructurada: cotización, endosos, FNOL, con validaciones y evidencia adjunta.
- Infra: definir SLOs de voz (p95/p99) y una estrategia de degradación (voz → texto) para no romper experiencia.
